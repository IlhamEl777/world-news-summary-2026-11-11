World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026. Russia and Ukraine concluded two days of US-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi, their first meeting in nine months, agreeing their largest prisoner exchange of the war — 1,000 to 1,500 prisoners a side under discussion, the figures to be finalised on Thursday — but no ceasefire; envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff next fly to Moscow and then Dublin (AP). Iran's parliament speaker, Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, told Al Jazeera the Strait of Hormuz will never return to its pre-war status, and that Tehran awaits a US reply to seven conditions sent through Qatar; Reuters reported October was the busiest month for Gulf LNG exports since the war began. World leaders opened the COP31 summit segment in Antalya, where host Turkey is pushing a deal to raise electricity's share of global energy use to 35% by 2035; UN Secretary-General António Guterres said the world was on a path to destruction, and the United States stayed away for a second year (Reuters). The US House voted 211-204 to refer impeachment articles against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to the Judiciary Committee, stalling the effort for the rest of this Congress (USA Today). Gaza's Health Ministry reported eight Palestinians killed by Israeli fire in 24 hours, as flooding hit displacement camps 13 months into a ceasefire each side accuses the other of breaching (Al Jazeera). British police said a 41-year-old US Army soldier based at NATO's Allied Rapid Reaction Corps headquarters in Gloucestershire was arrested on suspicion of spying for Russia, which the Kremlin denied (BBC). Sudan's army said it shot down two drones over West Kordofan and East Darfur, taking its claimed interception tally to 29, as its war with the Rapid Support Forces intensified (Sudan Now). The UN Human Rights Council postponed its review of the United States to January after Washington boycotted the process for a second year running (Al Jazeera).
